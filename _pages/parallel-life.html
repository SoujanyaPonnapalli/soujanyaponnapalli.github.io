Kuchipudi

I dance Kuchipudi, one of the eight classical dance forms of India, named for the village in Andhra Pradesh where it grew up. It began as theatre, so a piece is a story before it is a sequence of steps, carried by footwork, rhythm, and above all the face. Its signature item, the tarangam, is danced on the rim of a brass plate.

I trained at Nishrinkala Dance Academy in Hyderabad under Guru Sai Venkata Gangadhar, in the style of Padma Bhushan Vempati Chinna Satyam, who shaped the solo repertoire the form is danced in today.

These days I am building a community of Kuchipudi dancers in the Bay Area through Kuchipudi Meetups: we meet to practise together, and we host workshops, such as Anatomy of an Adugu with Katyayani Kanak, a closer look at adugus, posture, and technique.

I was away from the stage for sixteen years. I came back in April 2026 with Hindolam Thillana, a piece that demands strength and stamina, and I chose it deliberately. It was not perfect, and I am learning to be at peace with that.
